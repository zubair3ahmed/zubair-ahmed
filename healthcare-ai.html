Problem.

Before any UI

Agents lose five minutes of every call searching systems.

US healthcare contact centers handle 10,000+ calls a day across large hospital networks — and the data behind every call is scattered across disconnected systems the agent has to search live, mid-conversation.

10,000+ daily calls across large hospital networks — every one against a clock
4–5 min lost — manually verifying details while the patient waits on the line
3 silos EHR · scheduling · insurance portals — none of them talking

Research findings

01

Repetitive manual work dominates every shift — Scheduling, data entry, and refill requests — the same lookups, call after call.

02

Patient data is fragmented — Record, coverage, and availability live in separate silos — verified by hand, mid-call.

03

Cognitive load drives frustration and errors — Constant context switching between systems while holding a live conversation.

Contact-center agents

Searching multiple systems during live calls while managing queues and resources.

Patients

Long waits, avoidable errors, and the dissatisfaction that follows both.

Smarter assistance inside the tools agents already use — not new software to learn.

Cut booking time

04

Exploration.

Five places the AI could live

Before the panel, five directions — and why four failed. Each concept solved the previous one's worst flaw and introduced its own. The final design isn't a winner; it's an inheritance.

01

Floating Assistant

Rejected

Out of sight, out of mind. Time-sensitive guidance hidden behind a click the agent might never notice.

02

Step Wizard

Rejected

Conversations aren't linear. The modal covered the transcript and forced patients into a script they kept breaking.

03

Activity Timeline

Evolved

Right shape, read-only. A persistent side feed worked but it narrated the past instead of letting the agent act.

04

Chat Assistant

Evolved

Great voice, buried actions. Explainable and collaborative — but slots dissolved into prose the agent had to re-parse.

05

Embedded Panel

Shipped

The synthesis. Persistent like the timeline, conversational like chat — but every event is a structured, tappable action card.

05

Decisions.

Decision · Evidence · Outcome

Seven decisions that shaped the product. Not a tour of screens — a record of trade-offs. Each decision below traces from an observed failure to the shipped behavior it produced.

Decision 01

One persistent panel, docked beside the live call.

Why

Booking meant leaving the transcript for a separate scheduler — the exact context switch the product existed to remove.

Evidence

In every V1 session recording, the agent navigated away from the call at least once per booking.

Outcome

Intent → slot → confirm resolves in a single column. Zero tab switches across the full call lifecycle.

Decision 02

Silence until the AI has something to act on.

Why

V1's listening state showed a signal-strength bar and grayed-out suggestions. Agents compulsively checked it during HIPAA verification — the one moment that demands full attention on the patient.

Evidence

Session reviews showed eye-drift to the panel on every call open, before the AI had anything useful to say.

Outcome

The listening state is one quiet line. The empty state was the most important design decision in the product.

Decision 03

Intent as verification chips, not prose.

Why

V1 ran an intent strip across the top of the transcript — it created a "who do I look at?" moment between strip, transcript, and patient.

Evidence

Agents re-read prose summaries mid-call; chips were verified at a glance and clicked to override.

Outcome

Two zones, two purposes: glance right for AI state, left for patient words. The intent card scans in about two seconds.

Decision 04

A plain confidence bar — after testing four variants.

Why

Healthcare coordinators are trained to be skeptical. Confidence had to be legible without adding load.

Evidence

Tested bar vs. dots vs. chip vs. radial. Dots introduced counting load; the chip cost vertical height; the radial felt emphatic but unfamiliar.

Outcome

The bar mapped to a universal "progress" mental model — agents understood 96% instantly, no explanation needed.

Decision 05

Slots as tappable cards, with one deliberate confirm.

Why

In the chat direction, three appointment slots dissolved into a sentence the agent had to parse and re-type under call pressure.

Evidence

Dense prose was the slowest-scanning element in usability sessions; structured times were the fastest.

Outcome

Ranked cards with a best-match flag and tappable times. Booking is always one deliberate human click — never automatic.

Decision 06

A four-tier policy decides when the AI acts, suggests, or stays quiet.

Why

The bar agents see is the surface of a deeper question: when is the model allowed to pre-fill a booking, and when must it escalate?

Evidence

Threshold review with clinical stakeholders — uncertain clinical suggestions must never surface.

Outcome

A legible contract between model and agent. Behavior is predictable at every confidence level, so trust compounds.

Confidence Tier Behavior
92–100% HIGH Pre-fill the full booking. Human confirms with one tap.
70–91% MEDIUM Suggest with entity chips visible. Agent verifies first.
45–69% LOW Offer options, no pre-fill. The model learns from the choice.
<45% ESCALATE Stay quiet or route to a human. Never guess on clinical matters.
Decision 07

Same intent, three voices — a tone system for scripts.

Why

V1 shipped a single script per intent. Agents were rewriting it by hand, live, while the patient waited.

Evidence

Testing showed agents switched tone with the patient's emotional state — empathetic adoption jumped 3× when transcripts carried phrases like "I'm a little worried."

Outcome

Professional, empathetic, and concise variants per script. The AI picks the default from emotional signal in the transcript; the agent overrides with one tap.

06

AI UX.

What makes AI products different

Six challenges no SaaS playbook covers. Designing for a probabilistic system in a clinical setting raised problems that don't exist in deterministic software. Each one left a visible mark on the shipped product.

01

Trust

Agents won't repeat an AI suggestion to a patient they can't defend out loud. Trust is earned per interaction, never assumed.

Design move

AI proposes, the human confirms — every booking is one deliberate click, and nothing ever fires silently.

02

Transparency

A recommendation without a "why" gets ignored or overridden. The reasoning had to be visible without becoming homework.

Design move

Every suggestion carries its evidence — intent chips, confidence bar, and the transcript moment that triggered it.

03

Context Memory

Patients backtrack and change their minds mid-call. The AI's current belief had to stay inspectable — and correctable.

Design move

Entity chips show what the model currently believes; any chip can be tapped to override it on the spot.

04

Prompt Discoverability

A blank input box is a dead feature. Agents under call pressure won't invent prompts — capability has to surface itself.

Design move

The panel leads with state-aware suggested actions; the free-form composer is a fallback, not the front door.

05

AI Confidence

A raw percentage is meaningless without consequences. 96% of what? Confidence needed a behavioral contract.

Design move

The four-tier policy binds score to behavior — pre-fill, suggest, offer, or escalate. Agents learn the contract once.

06

Medical Safety

A wrong restaurant suggestion is a shrug. A wrong specialty referral is a harm event in a HIPAA-conscious environment.

Design move

Clinical topics hard-route to escalation regardless of confidence. The AI schedules; it never advises.

Live transcript with entity highlights on the left — the panel never covers or competes with it.

Inherited from the wizard's failure.

Intent chips, a confidence bar, and tappable slot cards in one prioritized column.

Chat's tone, the timeline's persistence.

Patient context on the right rail

Appointments and prescriptions sit one glance away — the data agents used to dig through three systems to find.

Composer as fallback, not front door

"Help me find answers…" handles the long tail. The default path is suggested actions, so capability surfaces itself.

Four states

One deliberate click books

The confirm is the single irreversible act, and it always belongs to the human.

The trust bet, kept.

Automations fire downstream

SMS reminder, clinical note, calendar entry, and follow-up task run in parallel — visible, so the agent ends the call clean.

The feed is the audit trail

Every AI action stays in the chronological feed — inspectable after the call, which is what made compliance comfortable.

09

V2.

What I'd build next

The shipped product is the floor, not the ceiling. Not lessons learned — a roadmap. Four investments I'd argue for next, in priority order.

V2—01

Long-term Patient Memory

The rolling context window resets every call. Cross-call memory — last visit, standing preferences, open follow-ups — would let the copilot start each call already oriented, instead of re-learning the patient live.

V2—02

Proactive Healthcare Workflows

Today the AI reacts to intent. The data already knows who's overdue for a follow-up or out of refills — the next step is the copilot opening the call with the work pre-staged, before the patient asks.

V2—03

Per-agent Personalization

Tone defaults, suggestion density, and confirm friction are global today. Agents differ — a five-year veteran and a first-week hire shouldn't get the same level of hand-holding from the panel.

V2—04

Explainability Beyond the Score

The confidence bar says how sure; it doesn't say why. An evidence trace — the transcript moments that drove the classification — would turn the bar from a claim into an argument.
